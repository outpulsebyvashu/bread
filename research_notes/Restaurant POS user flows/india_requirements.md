# India requirements shaping restaurant POS flows (status as of 5 Nov 2026)

This informs breadPOS product design. It is not legal or tax advice. A practising CA or lawyer should confirm items before they are hard-coded.

**How to read the labels**
- **[official]** means a government, regulator or court source. **[secondary]** means a practitioner, law firm, vendor, professional body or press source.
- **(read)** means I read the document itself. **(excerpt)** means I only saw a search excerpt or summary, so the wording is less certain. Search summaries did not always tie a claim to one URL, so for (excerpt) items the link is the best match from that result set and should be opened before the claim is relied on.
- **LAW** marks a legal requirement and **PRACTICE** marks common practice. **Inferences** are my own reading.
- The CBIC repository (taxinformation.cbic.gov.in) would not load from this environment because of a certificate error. Rule text is therefore from search excerpts of CBIC pages, or from reproductions in other documents. No current consolidated copy of Rules 46, 49, 53 or 56, or of Notification 11/2017, was read directly.

## GST on restaurant bills: rates, composition, inclusive vs exclusive pricing, ITC

### Takeaway
A standalone cafe or restaurant charges 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) with no input tax credit (ITC) and cannot opt for 18%. Only restaurants in "specified premises" (hotels with a room above ₹7,500 per unit per day) charge 18% with ITC. Composition (up to ₹1.5 crore) means 5% paid by the business, no tax collected from the customer, and a bill of supply. The 57th GST Council (8 Oct 2026) recommended limited ITC for restaurants, but I found no notification, so it is not law yet.

### Cited Findings

**Rates**
- LAW: Restaurant service outside specified premises is 5% without ITC under entry 7(ii) of Notification 11/2017-CTR. At specified premises it is 18% with ITC under entry 7(vi). Effective in this form from 1 Apr 2025. — [official] (read) [CBIC FAQ on restaurant service at specified premises, state GST copy, Mar 2025](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/GSTFAQ28325.pdf). The 28 Mar 2025 date comes from the [TaxO report](https://taxo.online/latest-news/28-03-2025-cbic-issued-faqs-on-gst-structure-on-hotel-accommodation-service/) [secondary] (excerpt).
- LAW: From 1 Apr 2025, "specified premises" for a financial year (FY) means one of the following. — [official] (read) [same FAQ](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/GSTFAQ28325.pdf)
  - A premises that supplied hotel accommodation above ₹7,500 per unit per day in the preceding FY.
  - A premises declared as specified between 1 Jan and 31 Mar of the preceding FY.
  - A premises declared by a new registration applicant within 15 days of the application acknowledgement.
- LAW: Status is fixed for the FY and cannot change during it. Restaurants in a mall but outside the declared hotel premises stay at 5% without ITC. — [official] (read) [same FAQ](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/GSTFAQ28325.pdf)
- LAW (recommendation reported): The 56th Council added an explanation that a standalone restaurant cannot classify itself as specified premises to opt for 18% with ITC. Service rate changes took effect 22 Sep 2025. — [secondary] (read) [KPMG Flash News, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.in.kpmg.com/taxflashnews/KPMG-Flash-News-Recommendations-of-56th-GST-Council-meeting.pdf). The notification text itself was not read.
- Restaurants outside specified premises were confirmed at 5% without ITC after GST 2.0. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=965018), date not captured. One vendor page still shows 18% for SAC 996331 and looks outdated: [LS Central](https://help.lscentral.lsretail.com/Content/Localization/IN/Restaurant-GST.htm) [secondary] (excerpt).
- Takeaway, room service and door delivery count as restaurant service, per Circular 164/20/2021-GST (6 Oct 2021). — [secondary] (read) [CA Surekha Ahuja, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html). The circular itself was not read.

**Items a cafe or bakery may sell as goods (Council rates effective 22 Sep 2025)**
- Pastry, cakes and bakers' wares 18% to 5%. Ice cream 18% to 5%. Namkeens 12% to 5%. Plain water 18% to 5%. Plant-based milk drinks 18% to 5%. Fruit-juice-based drinks (non-carbonated) 12% to 5%. — [secondary] (read) [KPMG, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.in.kpmg.com/taxflashnews/KPMG-Flash-News-Recommendations-of-56th-GST-Council-meeting.pdf)
- Sugared or flavoured aerated drinks 28% to 40%. "Other non-alcoholic beverages" under 2202 91/99 18% to 40%. — [secondary] (read) [same KPMG note](https://www.in.kpmg.com/taxflashnews/KPMG-Flash-News-Recommendations-of-56th-GST-Council-meeting.pdf)
- Goods-versus-restaurant-service treatment at bakery-cafes is contested and fact-specific.
  - One Odisha appellate ruling says dine-in versus takeaway does not change restaurant-service character: [TMI](https://taxmanagementindia.com/web/tmi_highlights_details.asp?id=84547) [secondary] (excerpt; which link carries this ruling is not certain).
  - A Goa AAR treats ready-made bakery items as goods and asks for separate records: [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/legal/news/sale-of-readymade-bakery-products-constitute-supply-says-goa-aar-8efac39d-b4fd-4a33-ab8c-acf380978e29/) [secondary] (excerpt).
  - Bought-out items attract goods tax with ITC: [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=142354) [secondary] (excerpt).
  - A Bombay High Court challenge on donuts and cakes was reported: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/tax-cases/bombay-high-court-decide-donuts-cakes-should-classified-restaurant-service-bakery-product-under-gst-285767) [secondary] (excerpt). Outcome not found.

**Alcohol**
- LAW: Alcoholic liquor for human consumption is outside GST and falls under State VAT and excise. It is reported in GSTR-1 Table 8 as non-GST. — [secondary] (read) [TaxGuru, 11 Jul 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057717). The article's service-charge illustration is questionable (see the service charge section).

**Composition scheme**
- LAW: Restaurants with prior-year aggregate turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in special category states) may opt in, under section 10(1). — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=710969), date not captured. Some older pages still say ₹50 lakh and are stale: [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=962107).
- LAW: The rate is 5% of turnover (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST). — [secondary] (excerpt) [TMI manual](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_manual.asp?ID=2745)
- LAW: A composition dealer cannot collect tax from customers or claim ITC, and the option lapses from the day turnover crosses the limit. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=963880)
- LAW: A composition dealer issues a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice. — [official] (read) [NACIN/CBIC flyer on tax invoices, undated](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf)
- A new item to watch: the 57th Council approved in principle an optional Annual Return Quarterly Payment scheme for B2C-only taxpayers with turnover up to ₹5 crore. It is a concept note only and affects returns, not bills. — [official] (read) [PIB, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2320934&reg=48&lang=2)

**ITC**
- LAW: Today, 5% restaurant service carries no ITC. — [official] (read) [CBIC FAQ](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/GSTFAQ28325.pdf)
- PROPOSED: The 57th Council recommended "limited input tax credit in the same line of business" for restaurant and outdoor catering services. — [official] (read) [PIB, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2320934&reg=48&lang=2)
- PROPOSED: This is about the section 17(5) block and is "not self-executing", so taxpayers should not claim on the announcement alone. — [secondary] (read) [TaxGuru, 14 Oct 2026](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/blocked-itc-section-17-5-57th-gst-council.html). Effective date unspecified: [Trilegal, 15 Oct 2026](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/analysis/key-recommendations-of-the-57th-gst-council-meeting/) [secondary] (read). It is not general credit for rent, advertising or kitchen equipment, per coverage summarised in a [TaxGuru table](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/blocked-itc-section-17-5-57th-gst-council.html) [secondary] (excerpt).

**Inclusive vs exclusive pricing**
- LAW: An invoice must show taxable value after discount, tax rate and tax amount by tax head (Rule 46(k), (l), (m)). The source is Rule 46 as reproduced in a Jan 2024 opinion, so later amendments may not be reflected. — [secondary reproduction] (read) [UBR Legal opinion for HRAWI, 31 Jan 2024](https://www.hrawi.com/(S(ly30fwqswj00vq13z1w5q1hh))/assets/pdf/Circular%20regarding%20E-Invoicing%20-%20Applicability%20of%20E-Invoicing.pdf)
- I found no source that mandates either inclusive or exclusive menu pricing. See Gaps.

### Inferences
- Make tax configuration outlet-level and FY-locked: `regular_5_no_itc`, `specified_premises_18_itc`, `composition_5`. A standalone outlet should not be offered 18%.
- Support per-item tax classes so a bakery-cafe can separate prepared food from bought-out or packaged goods. Do not auto-classify. Let the merchant's CA set the mapping. A packaged soft drink is the risky case, because 40% (goods) and 5% (restaurant service) can both look plausible.
- Support non-GST or VAT items on their own line and report bucket for licensed outlets.
- Either pricing display can work if the POS back-calculates taxable value for inclusive prices. Discounts must reduce taxable value before tax.
- In composition mode, the POS should not show a tax line or any wording implying tax is collected.
- The 57th Council ITC proposal looks like a purchase-side matter, so I would not change sales flows for it. Keep tax rules data-driven in case the rate entry wording changes.

### Gaps
- Could not read the notification that implements the 56th Council standalone-restaurant explanation, or the current text of entry 7 of Notification 11/2017.
- No confirmed effective date for the 57th Council ITC change, and no sign yet of whether the "without ITC" wording in entry 7(ii) will be amended. Some coverage expects around April 2027, but the Trilegal note ties April 2027 only to returns-related changes.
- Not verified this session, from memory only: section 10(2) bars composition for suppliers of goods not leviable to GST, which would rule out composition for outlets selling alcohol. Restaurant service is place-of-supply at the restaurant location, so bills are normally CGST + SGST and not IGST. Both need checking.
- No source found on whether menus legally must state tax-inclusive prices, or how Legal Metrology MRP rules apply to packaged items sold in restaurants. The Delhi High Court questioned ₹100 water bottles in hearing remarks, which is not a ruling: [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/litigation/service-charge-on-top-of-100-rupee-water-bottle-delhi-high-court-miffed) [secondary] (excerpt).

## What a valid tax invoice or bill of supply must show

### Takeaway
Every bill needs the supplier's name, address and GSTIN, a consecutive serial of up to 16 characters unique within the FY, date, description, taxable value and tax by head. The 14-digit FSSAI number must also appear. Customer details are only mandatory for registered buyers (GSTIN) or for unregistered buyers at ₹50,000 and above. There is no prescribed format.

### Cited Findings
- LAW: Rule 46 particulars. — [secondary reproduction] (read) [UBR Legal opinion quoting Rule 46, 31 Jan 2024](https://www.hrawi.com/(S(ly30fwqswj00vq13z1w5q1hh))/assets/pdf/Circular%20regarding%20E-Invoicing%20-%20Applicability%20of%20E-Invoicing.pdf)
  - (a) supplier name, address, GSTIN
  - (b) serial number
  - (c) date of issue
  - (d) recipient name, address and GSTIN/UIN if registered
  - (e) and (f) unregistered recipient's name, address, delivery address and state, if value is ₹50,000 or more, or if the recipient asks below that
  - (g) HSN/SAC
  - (h) description
  - (i) quantity and unit for goods
  - (j) total value
  - (k) taxable value net of discount
  - (l) and (m) rate and amount by CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST, cess
  - (n) place of supply for inter-state supplies
  - (o) delivery address if different
  - (p) reverse charge indicator
  - (q) signature or digital signature
  - (r) IRN QR code where e-invoiced
  - (s) a declaration in some non-e-invoice cases (see the e-invoicing section)
- LAW: Serial rule, verbatim: "a consecutive serial number not exceeding sixteen characters, in one or multiple series, containing alphabets or numerals or special characters- hyphen or dash and slash ... unique for a financial year". — [official] (excerpt) [CBIC CGST Rules, Rule 49 page and repository](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter6/rule49_v1.00.html)
- LAW: A bill of supply needs the same serial rule, and Rule 46 provisos apply to it. — [official] (excerpt) [CBIC Rule 49](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter6/rule49_v1.00.html). Contents include name, address, GSTIN and serial: [NACIN flyer](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf) [official] (read).
- LAW: A composition dealer must print "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" at the top of the bill of supply and show "COMPOSITION TAXABLE PERSON" on signboards. — [official] (excerpt) [Maharashtra trade circular via TMI](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/Detail_Circular.asp?ID=61761), [Rule 5 via GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/a/conditions-and-restrictions-for-composition-levy-cgst-rule-5.html) [secondary] (excerpt). Sources date from 2017 to 2019, and the exact wording and comma placement were not confirmed against the current rule.
- LAW: Below ₹200 a registered person may skip the invoice if the recipient is unregistered and does not require one, but must issue a consolidated tax invoice "at the close of each day". — [official] (read) [NACIN flyer](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf). If the customer asks, or if the value is ₹200 or more, the relaxation does not apply: [Cleartax](https://cleartax.in/s/minimum-invoice-value) [secondary] (excerpt).
- LAW: There is no prescribed invoice format. — [official] (read) [NACIN flyer](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf)
- LAW: HSN/SAC digits under Notification 78/2020-CT (effective 1 Apr 2021). Above ₹5 crore, 6 digits on all invoices. Up to ₹5 crore, 4 digits on B2B and optional on B2C. — [secondary] (excerpt) [IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/for-taxpayer-with-turnover-upto-rs-5-cr-mention-4-digit-hsn-code-and-for-turnover-above-rs-5-cr-mention-6-digit-hsn-code/), [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=97083). The notification text was not read.
- SAC 996331 covers restaurants, cafes and similar eating facilities, including takeaway, room service and door delivery. — [secondary] (excerpt) [Busy](https://busy.in/sac-code-996331)
- LAW: Above ₹500 crore turnover, B2C invoices need a dynamic QR (Notification 14/2020-CT). A dynamic QR on a digital display with payment cross-reference is deemed compliant. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=84580), [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/dynamic-quick-response-qr-code.html). Later waivers not confirmed.
- LAW (FSSAI): All food businesses must show the 14-digit licence or registration number on cash receipts, invoices, cash memos and bills. System-generated government documents such as e-way bills are exempt. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/fssai-food-businesses-mention-license-no-invoices-w-e-f-1st-october-2021.html), [SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/fssai-license-no-mandatory-receipts-restaurants/). The FSSAI order itself was not read.
- Effective date conflict for the FSSAI rule: most sources give an 8 Jun 2021 order effective 1 Oct 2021. A later account says an order of 13 Sep 2022 refers to "with effect from 1st January 2022". — [secondary] (read) [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/news/fssai-order-mandatory-declaration-of-fssai-license-registration-number-on-cash-receipts-invoices-cash-memo-bills-etc-by-all-food-businesses/). Either way it has been in force for years.
- Print the granted licence number, not the application number. Customers can verify it through FSSAI's portal or the Food Safety Connect app. — [secondary] (excerpt) [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/?p=47399), [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting/fssai-registration-number-on-invoices-mandatory-from-1st-october/)

### Inferences
- Numbering: outlet-prefixed series per document type (tax invoice, bill of supply, credit note), max 16 characters, reset or new prefix on 1 April, with the rule enforced in code. The ₹200 consolidated route is not worth building as a default, because a POS can cheaply print every bill. Printing all bills as serial tax invoices avoids an end-of-day consolidation step.
- A customer phone number must never be a required field for billing. Only GSTIN capture for B2B, and name and address at ₹50,000+, have a legal basis.
- Multi-terminal and offline billing need a design that guarantees uniqueness and avoids gaps, such as a per-device series. Rule 46 expressly allows multiple series.
- Setup should block going live without GSTIN (unless unregistered) and FSSAI number, and should print SAC even where optional.
- Printing a QR for payment is separate from the GST dynamic QR rule, except for chains above ₹500 crore.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed against current consolidated Rule 46 text.
- Not established whether a signature is needed on a printed thermal bill. A digital signature or authorised-signatory line is the usual reading, but I found no restaurant-specific official guidance.
- How an unregistered small cafe (below the registration threshold) must bill: it issues no GST document, and the FSSAI requirement still applies. Threshold not researched.

## E-invoicing, cancelled or edited invoices, credit notes, audit trail

### Takeaway
Restaurants are not exempt from e-invoicing. If aggregate turnover exceeded ₹5 crore in any FY since 2017-18, B2B bills to GST-registered customers need an IRN, and B2C bills do not. Issued invoices should be treated as immutable: GST Rule 56(8) requires a log of every edited or deleted electronic entry for all registered persons, and companies also need non-disableable audit trails under the Companies Act rules.

### Cited Findings

**E-invoicing**
- LAW: The threshold is ₹5 crore from 1 Aug 2023 (Notification 10/2023-CT of 10 May 2023, amending 13/2020-CT). — [secondary] (excerpt) [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/?p=136519), [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/e-invoice-from-august-2023/)
- Legal opinion: The notification covers only supplies to registered persons. A restaurant charging 5% above ₹5 crore must e-invoice for customers who give a GSTIN, "irrespective of the fact whether credit is available or not". The threshold is read as any FY preceding. — [secondary] (read) [UBR Legal opinion for HRAWI, 31 Jan 2024](https://www.hrawi.com/(S(ly30fwqswj00vq13z1w5q1hh))/assets/pdf/Circular%20regarding%20E-Invoicing%20-%20Applicability%20of%20E-Invoicing.pdf). This is a private-circulation opinion, though publicly reachable. It also records that GST authorities sent notices to hotels and restaurants on this point in Jan 2024.
- LAW: Exempt classes I saw listed are SEZ units, insurers, banks and financial institutions (rule 54(2) to (4A)), government departments and local authorities. Restaurants were not in those excerpts, but I did not see the complete current list. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxReply copy of 13/2020](https://taxreply.com/gstnotifications-html/Notification-No-13-2020-1307.html), [CA Kanhu's blog, 29 Dec 2025](https://cakanhus.blogspot.com/2025/12/comprehensive-summary-of-gst-e.html) (that blog misstates the original threshold, so use cautiously).
- LAW: Invoices, credit notes and debit notes must be reported to the IRP within 30 days if AATO is ₹10 crore or more, from 1 Apr 2025. Below that, "no such restriction ... as of now" per the 2024 wording. — [official] (excerpt) [GSTN advisory of 5 Nov 2024, Maharashtra GST copy](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1760531580_293%20Advisory%20Time%20Limit%20for%20Reporting%20e%20Invoice%20on%20the%20IRP%20Portal%20%20Lowering%20of%20Threshold%20to%20AATO%2010%20Crores%20and%20Above.pdf), [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/time-limit-of-30-days-for-reporting-e-invoice-on-irp-portal-for-taxpayers-with-aato-of-10-crores-and-above-gstn-update/) [secondary] (excerpt). Whether this later dropped below ₹10 crore was not checked.
- Rule 46(s) requires a declaration on non-e-invoice invoices by taxpayers above the threshold in some cases. The text reads broadly: "we are not required to prepare an invoice in terms of the provisions of the said sub-rule". — [secondary reproduction] (read) [UBR opinion quoting Rule 46](https://www.hrawi.com/(S(ly30fwqswj00vq13z1w5q1hh))/assets/pdf/Circular%20regarding%20E-Invoicing%20-%20Applicability%20of%20E-Invoicing.pdf)
- PROPOSED: E-invoicing to be extended to RCM purchases from unregistered persons and import of services at ₹5 crore and above. Purchase side, not yet in force. — [official] (read) [PIB, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2320934&reg=48&lang=2)

**Cancellation, edits, credit notes**
- LAW: Rule 56(8), verbatim: "Any entry in registers, accounts and documents shall not be erased, effaced or overwritten, and all incorrect entries, otherwise than those of clerical nature, shall be scored out under attestation and there after the correct entry shall be recorded and where the registers and other documents are maintained electronically, a log of every entry edited or deleted shall be maintained." — [official] (excerpt) [CBIC Rule 56](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter7/rule56_v1.00.html)
- LAW: A credit note reducing output tax must be declared by 30 Nov following the FY of supply, or the annual return date if earlier. For FY 2025-26 that was 30 Nov 2026. After that, a credit note can still be issued commercially but without tax adjustment. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru on section 34](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-34-understanding-credit-notes-gst.html), [The Tax Corp](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/gst-year-end-compliance-action-points-for-fy-2025-26-itc-credit-notes-rcm).
- Warning: The older CBIC flyer still says "September". It predates the change and should not be used for this date. — [official] (read) [NACIN flyer](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf)
- LAW: No reduction in output tax is permitted if the tax incidence was passed on to another person. — [official] (read) [NACIN flyer](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Tax_Invoice_and_other_new.pdf)
- PROPOSED: Rule 60(6A) to give the Invoice Management System statutory backing, with returns-related changes proposed from the April 2027 return. Relevant to B2B credit notes, where recipients may accept, reject or hold them. — [secondary] (read) [Trilegal, 15 Oct 2026](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/analysis/key-recommendations-of-the-57th-gst-council-meeting/)

**Audit trail**
- LAW: The proviso to Rule 3(1), Companies (Accounts) Rules, requires a company keeping books electronically to use only software that records an audit trail of each transaction, creates an edit log with dates, and cannot have the trail disabled. Notified 24 Mar 2021. — [secondary] (excerpt) [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=763573). Applicable from FY 2023-24 after deferrals (sources differ on earlier dates): [TaxGuru practitioner guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html) [secondary] (excerpt).
- LAW: It covers all companies regardless of size, with no turnover threshold found. Auditors report under Rule 11(g). — [secondary] (excerpt) [MBG Corp](https://www.mbgcorp.com/in/insights/key-requirements-of-the-mca-audit-trail-rule-issued-by-mca/)
- ICAI reads section 128(5) as requiring the audit trail to be kept at least eight years from 1 Apr 2023. Accounting software can be cloud or SaaS, and includes service organisation systems. Management identifies "applications, web-portals, databases, interfaces" used to create books. — [secondary, ICAI guidance] (read) [ICAI Revised 2024 Implementation Guide](https://www.eirc-icai.org/uploads/background_materials/Revised%202024_Implementation%20Guide%20on%20Reporting%20of%20Audit%20Trail%20(1)_1712114860.pdf)
- ICAI says feeder systems may count: registers with "direct and auto feed" to accounting software should be treated as part of books, and the same assessment "would also hold good for sales order and price master", depending on judgement. A text search of the guide found no mention of POS or billing systems. — [secondary, ICAI guidance] (read) [same guide](https://www.eirc-icai.org/uploads/background_materials/Revised%202024_Implementation%20Guide%20on%20Reporting%20of%20Audit%20Trail%20(1)_1712114860.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lifecycle: draft or open order (freely editable, not a tax document) leads to issued invoice (immutable). Corrections after issue go through a credit note, or a linked void with reason, user and timestamp. Never overwrite or delete.
- Same-day void vs credit note: I found no provision that defines "void". Keep the number, mark it cancelled, and never reuse it. Let a CA choose the policy per merchant, with a credit note as the safe default after the return period.
- Run a timestamped log for edits to invoices and price master. Treat POS as potentially in-scope for company merchants, since sales feed the books. Retain logs for at least 8 years and do not offer a disable switch.
- E-invoice: capture GSTIN on the bill. If the outlet is above the threshold and the GSTIN is present, block final print until IRN returns, or queue with a visible 30-day clock. Offline handling matters. Keep the B2C flow free of any IRN step.
- Add a 30 Nov credit-note cutoff warning for prior-FY bills.

### Gaps
- From memory, not verified this session: IRN cancellation window of 24 hours on the IRP. GSTR-1 Table 13 reports document series and cancelled counts. Section 36 retention is 72 months. Each should be checked.
- No official source on whether a same-day pre-payment void is permissible without a credit note.
- No authority found on whether a standalone POS is "accounting software" under Rule 3(1). LLPs and proprietorships appear outside the rule because it is worded for companies, but no source says so expressly.
- Whether a restaurant above the threshold must print the Rule 46(s) declaration on B2C bills is unclear. Ask a CA.

## Service charge and rounding

### Takeaway
Do not auto-add service charge under any name. A January 2026 CCPA order directed one restaurant to change its billing software. The March 2025 Delhi High Court judgment upholding the guidelines is under appeal, and I found no Division Bench decision or stay. Rounding has no restaurant-specific rule, and a labelled round-off line is common practice.

### Cited Findings
- LAW: CCPA Guidelines of 4 Jul 2022: (1) no automatic or default addition; (2) no collection under any other name; (3) no forcing, and consumers must be clearly told it is voluntary and optional; (4) no denial of entry or service on refusal; (5) "Service charge shall not be added to the bill and subjected to GST". — [official] (read) [PIB/Dept of Consumer Affairs press release, 10 Jan 2026](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/pressRelease/Mandatory_Levy_of_Service_Charge_by_Restaurants_Violates_Consumer_Law_CCPApress_release.pdf)
- LAW: The Delhi High Court on 28 Mar 2025 upheld the guidelines, held mandatory collection contrary to law, and affirmed that CCPA may enforce them. — [official] (read) [same release](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/pressRelease/Mandatory_Levy_of_Service_Charge_by_Restaurants_Violates_Consumer_Law_CCPApress_release.pdf). Justice Prathiba M Singh imposed ₹1 lakh costs: [Bar and Bench, 28 Mar 2025](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/restaurants-cant-levy-mandatory-service-charge-in-food-bills-delhi-high-court) [secondary] (read).
- Enforcement: action against 27 restaurants as of 10 Jan 2026. Café Blue Bottle, Patna: refund plus ₹30,000. China Gate (Bora Bora), Mumbai: ₹50,000 and "Modify its software-generated billing system to remove default addition of service charge or any similar charge". — [official] (read) [same release](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/pressRelease/Mandatory_Levy_of_Service_Charge_by_Restaurants_Violates_Consumer_Law_CCPApress_release.pdf)
- Enforcement later grew to 41 restaurants. Chaayos fined ₹50,000, refund ordered, and billing software changes directed. — [secondary] (read) [Upstox, updated 20 Jul 2026](https://www.upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/ccpa-cracks-down-on-41-restaurants-over-default-service-charge-what-do-guidelines-say/article-197172/)
- A further CCPA order of 4 Feb 2026 against Barbeque Nation is reported. — [secondary] (excerpt) [Supreme Today](https://supremetoday.ai/ccpa-directs-barbecue-nation-stop-mandatory-service-charge-20260210028)
- Appeal status: NRAI and FHRAI appealed to a Division Bench (National Restaurant Association v. Union of India). Listed 9 May 2025, then 23 May 2025, with no stay reported in that article. — [secondary] (read) [LiveLaw, 9 May 2025](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-restaurant-mandatory-service-charge-291763). I found no later order in my searches.
- Conflict on tax treatment: a Jul 2026 practitioner article still lists service charge as taxable and includes it in Table 7. — [secondary] (read) [TaxGuru, 11 Jul 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057717). That sits awkwardly with guideline 5, and the article also applies service charge on liquor inside its taxable value without explanation.
- Rounding: LAW: Section 170 CGST rounds tax and other sums payable under the Act to the nearest rupee, with 50 paise or more rounded up. — [secondary reproduction] (excerpt) [KnowYourGST](https://www.knowyourgst.com/gstlaw/cgst-act/170-rounding-off-of-tax-etc-170/). It concerns amounts payable and is not a blanket direction to round each invoice line: [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1088943) [secondary] (excerpt).
- Rounding: PRACTICE: Bills commonly round to the nearest rupee with a "round off" line. The only evidence I found is a user discussion, so treat it as weak. — [secondary, low quality] (excerpt) [Reddit snapshot, Apr 2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sjniit/snapshots/2026-04-17T20%3A24%3A27.152189Z)

### Inferences
- Service charge should default to off, never be auto-applied at outlet, table-size or order-type level, and offer no renamed equivalents such as "staff contribution" or "convenience fee" as automatic lines.
- If offered at all, make it a tip captured after the bill total, at the customer's choice, outside the tax computation and tax invoice. Print voluntary wording. A CA should confirm the GST treatment of voluntary tips.
- Treat the rule as nationwide. CCPA is acting across states on the strength of the guidelines, even though the judgment is from the Delhi High Court.
- Rounding: calculate tax per line or per bill with paise precision, keep one consistent method, and show round-off as a separate non-taxable line. Round-off is a merchant setting, and UPI or card amounts can be exact to the paisa. Store both exact and rounded totals so reconciliation against the gateway amount works. The project's UI convention of showing whole rupees without `.00` (`/home/vashudev/bread/CLAUDE.md`) should stay display-only: bills with paise must still show them, and stored values must keep them.

### Gaps
- Current status of the Division Bench appeal could not be confirmed (no cause-list access). Check before launch.
- Whether a voluntary tip paid via the POS is part of the value of supply is unresolved in the sources I found.
- No official guidance found on POS rounding of taxable value or line tax.

## UPI at the counter

### Takeaway
There is no found legal requirement to use dynamic QR, so the choice is about reconciliation. A per-bill dynamic QR with server-side confirmation (webhook plus polling) ties payment to invoice. A static QR plus soundbox tells staff only that some amount arrived. The flow must treat "pending" as a real state, because RBI's outer limit for reversing a merchant UPI payment without confirmation is T+5.

### Cited Findings

**Dynamic QR and confirmation (vendor documentation)**
- PRACTICE: A QR is generated per payment by creating a virtual account with `receivers.types: qr_code`. `amount_expected` is described as the maximum amount expected. `close_by` must be at least 15 minutes after creation, and manual close is available. Status can be confirmed by polling or webhooks. A payment shows `captured` when paid. Fields exist for `amount_refunded` and `refund_status`. — [secondary, vendor docs] (read) [Razorpay UPI QR API](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/upi-qr/api.md), undated
- PRACTICE: A dynamic soundbox takes the amount from billing software, shows a unique QR per invoice on a customer-facing screen, confirms with voice and on-screen status, and matches transactions to invoices automatically. The page's claim that failed UPI transactions are "refunded instantly" is marketing and should not be relied on. — [secondary, vendor docs] (read) [Razorpay Dynamic Soundbox](https://razorpay.com/docs/pos/dynamic-soundbox.md), undated

**NPCI network rules**
- LAW-like (scheme rule on banks and PSPs): NPCI circular of 26 Apr 2025, compliance by 31 Jul 2025. The first check-transaction-status call comes only after 90 seconds, with at most 3 calls in 2 hours. Status and reversal API response times tightened from 30 to 10 seconds. Peak-hour limits apply to non-customer-initiated calls. — [secondary] (excerpt) [Business Standard, 22 May 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/npci-tightens-upi-api-supervision-to-curb-network-stress-125052201406_1.html), [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/banks-told-to-audit-upi-systems-follow-new-api-usage-norms-amid-outages-npci-circular/article-164050). One secondary source reports different windows (45 to 60 seconds, 5:30 pm evening start), and the circular itself was not read.
- Merchant collect requests are being phased out in favour of customer push payments. — [secondary] (excerpt) [Business Standard, Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-collect-call-transactions-phase-out-npci-fraud-125031800575_1.html). Completion not confirmed.
- Unverified offline merchants have a ₹2,000 cap on QR "share and pay" (image-based, not live scan), per an 8 Apr 2025 circular. Live scanning of physical QR codes still works. — [secondary] (excerpt) [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/no-more-international-upi-transactions-via-qr-share-pay-says-npci)
- Higher P2M limits (up to ₹10 lakh a day) for selected verified categories from 15 Sep 2025. Restaurants were not confirmed to be included. — [official] (excerpt) [News On AIR, 12 Sep 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/npci-announces-new-upi-p2m-transaction-limit-to-take-effect-september-15)

**Failure and dispute timelines**
- LAW: RBI circular RBI/2019-20/67 of 20 Sep 2019. T is the calendar date of the transaction. UPI payment to merchant (account debited but merchant confirmation not received): auto-reversal within T+5, then ₹100 per day. UPI fund transfer (beneficiary not credited): auto-reversal by T+1, then ₹100 per day. These are outer limits. — [official] (read) [RBI circular](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693). A later revision was not found, and a Jul 2026 explainer restates the same rules: [Tech2](https://www.tech2.com/tech2/upi-transaction-failed-heres-how-to-seek-compensation-as-per-rbis-rule-text-ws-l-10225991.html) [secondary] (excerpt).
- UDIR is NPCI's automated dispute framework. If the beneficiary bank or merchant does not respond within 3 days of a P2M transaction, the complaint can auto-convert to a chargeback, and NPCI polls up to 5 days for status. — [secondary, vendor] (excerpt) [Razorpay on UDIR](https://razorpay.com/blog/all-you-need-to-know-about-npci-led-udir/). Check against NPCI.
- PhonePe tells merchants they have 7 working days to respond to a chargeback with proof. — [secondary, vendor marketing] (excerpt) [PhonePe](https://business.phonepe.com/articles/how-to-defend-your-business-against-chargebacks-friendly-fraud). Timing differs from the item above and should be confirmed per acquirer.

### Inferences
Suggested flow, not a regulatory requirement.
- **Happy path:** bill locked, dynamic QR with exact amount and invoice reference, webhook marks it paid, print and close. Add a polling fallback in case the webhook is lost. Use gateway APIs with their rate limits and do not call too aggressively, in line with the NPCI status-call spirit.
- **Pending state:** show "Waiting", then "Pending, not confirmed" after a timeout. Offer the cashier: keep waiting, try again with a new QR, or switch to cash or card. Warn about the double-payment risk.
- **Late success:** a QR may remain open for at least 15 minutes under Razorpay's model. On switching tender, close the QR explicitly and handle any late credit as an unmatched payment for refund or reallocation.
- **Do not trust the customer's phone screen:** the only "paid" signal should be from the gateway or provider. Staff override should require a reason and be logged, with an exception report.
- **Static QR:** support as a low-cost mode with manual "mark as paid" plus soundbox. Reference is weak, so reconciliation is by amount and time window against the provider statement. Flag as unverified until matched.
- **Reconciliation:** store gateway payment ID, UPI RRN, amount, time and invoice number. Daily report compares POS tender against gateway settlement, with buckets: matched, POS-only, gateway-only, refunded, disputed. Tender-level data should support dispute proof (invoice copy).
- **Refunds:** linked to a credit note or void, with a status that tracks provider confirmation.
- **Offline:** dynamic QR needs network. Fallback is static QR or cash with deferred verification.

### Gaps
- I did not read NPCI's QR specification, UDIR circulars, or TCC/RET response codes. NPCI's public pages did not surface them.
- No official source found on soundbox behaviour, on how long a soundbox alert lags, or on data about screenshot fraud. Vendor claims about eliminating it are unverified marketing.
- No official or vendor docs were reached for PhonePe or Paytm dynamic QR webhooks.
- No reliable data on the rate of pending or timeout transactions at restaurants.
- MDR and mandatory acceptance of digital payments under section 269SU were not researched.

## Aggregators: section 9(5) and how the POS should record orders

### Takeaway
For Swiggy and Zomato orders at non-specified premises, the platform, not the restaurant, pays GST at 5% on the food under section 9(5), in force since 1 Jan 2022. The POS must record these as a separate channel at gross value with no restaurant tax liability, and must keep them out of the direct-sales tax totals. Commissions are separate expenses, and restaurants in specified premises still pay their own tax.

### Cited Findings
- LAW: Notification 17/2021-CT(Rate) of 18 Nov 2021 brought restaurant service under section 9(5) from 1 Jan 2022. Circular 167/23/2021-GST (17 Dec 2021) clarifies. The restaurant does not charge GST on ECO-routed orders, the platform deposits it, and 1% section 52 TCS does not apply to this category. — [secondary] (read) [CA Surekha Ahuja, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html). Notification and circular not read directly.
- LAW: Exception: where a hotel unit exceeds ₹7,500 (specified premises), section 9(5) does not apply and the restaurant pays 18% with ITC. The source uses "declared tariff" wording, which the Apr 2025 definition replaced with value of supply. — [secondary] (read) [same source](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html); definition from [CBIC FAQ](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/GSTFAQ28325.pdf) [official] (read).
- LAW: Sweet shops and bakeries selling goods through a platform may fall under section 52 TCS instead, with the restaurant remaining liable. — [secondary] (read) [same source](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html)
- Reporting: restaurant reports ECO sales in GSTR-1 Table 14 with the ECO's GSTIN, and GSTR-3B Table 3.1.1(ii) as reporting only. Direct sales go in Table 7 (B2C) and 3.1(a). ECO reports in Table 15. — [secondary] (read) [CA Surekha Ahuja](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html), [TaxGuru, 11 Jul 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057717). Older guidance using Table 8 and 3.1(c) appears outdated: [HNA, Jan 2024](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/512b2-20thjan24_gst-return-disclosures-for-restaurants_tr.pdf) [secondary] (excerpt). No GSTN advisory was read.
- PRACTICE: The sale is recorded at gross value, and commission plus GST on commission is booked as separate expense, not netted. — [secondary] (read) [TaxGuru, 11 Jul 2026](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057717)
- Delivery fee: the 56th Council added local delivery via ECO to section 9(5) only where the person supplying delivery through the ECO is not registered, at 18% with ITC (SAC 996813), effective 22 Sep 2025. — [secondary] (read) [KPMG, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.in.kpmg.com/taxflashnews/KPMG-Flash-News-Recommendations-of-56th-GST-Council-meeting.pdf). Press framed this as Swiggy and Zomato being deemed suppliers of delivery: [YourStory, Sep 2025](https://yourstory.com/2025/09/gst-ruling-puts-delivery-fees-of-swiggy-zomato-under-new-tax) [secondary] (excerpt).
- PROPOSED: The 57th Council recommended amending section 9(5) to clarify ECO liability "irrespective of the business models". Also new simplified registration for small sellers on ECOs. — [official] (read) [PIB, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2320934&reg=48&lang=2)
- Composition conflict: section 10(2)(d) bars suppliers through an ECO that collects TCS. One view: restaurant service via 9(5) is not TCS, so eligibility survives, with non-uniform departmental positions. — [secondary] (read) [CA Surekha Ahuja](https://www.casahuja.com/2026/09/gst-on-restaurant-cloud-kitchen-sales.html); also a [TMI forum thread](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119786) [secondary] (excerpt). Contrary view: aggregator-listed kitchens are generally disqualified: [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-for-cloud-kitchens-in-india/) [secondary] (excerpt).

### Inferences
- Order channel is a first-class field: `dine_in`, `takeaway`, `own_delivery`, `eco:<name>` with ECO GSTIN.
- ECO order record: platform order ID, gross item value, restaurant-funded and platform-funded discount, tax paid by platform as info, commission, GST on commission, TDS/TCS if any, and expected payout.
- ECO orders should not consume the tax-invoice series. Use an internal order number. Print KOT and packing slip, without a restaurant tax line.
- Own website or WhatsApp orders taken directly use normal invoice flow, with tax charged by the restaurant. Verify if an own-app vendor acts as an ECO.
- Reports must produce: direct taxable sales and CGST/SGST; ECO gross by platform GSTIN (Table 14 feed); non-GST liquor. Also a payout reconciliation matching order to settlement and flagging short-pays and cancellations.
- Outlets in specified premises flip the rule: an ECO order carries the restaurant's own 18% liability. So the ECO rule must depend on outlet tax configuration.
- Warn in composition setup if an aggregator channel is enabled, and point to a CA.

### Gaps
- Who must issue what document to the customer for ECO orders, and whether the restaurant must also issue anything, was not confirmed from the circular text. One source says the ECO could issue the invoice: [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-for-cloud-kitchens-in-india/) [secondary] (excerpt).
- Whether ECO-routed turnover counts toward the restaurant's aggregate turnover (for the ₹5 crore e-invoice or ₹1.5 crore composition limits) was not confirmed.
- The current GSTR-1 table scheme comes only from practitioner articles.
- Treatment of aggregator refunds and customer cancellations in the restaurant's books was not found.

## Customer phone numbers and loyalty: DPDP Act and Rules

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified 13 Nov 2025, with main duties starting 13 May 2027 (some sources count 14 May). Build for them now. A phone number given for a receipt can be used for that receipt. Loyalty and marketing need separate, itemised notice and consent that is as easy to withdraw as to give. A proposal to shorten the timeline to 12 months (which would fall on 13 Nov 2026) had not been notified as of September 2026 and must be rechecked.

### Cited Findings
- LAW: Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 apply on publication. Rule 4 after one year. Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 after eighteen months. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules 2025, MeitY PDF, G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). The date and rule mapping are also in [Mirvo Legal, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/) [secondary] (read), which gives "about 13 May 2027". The [dcomply tracker](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/) [secondary] (read) gives 14 May 2027.
- Current status: MeitY floated cutting the 18 months to 12 in Jan 2026, with feedback to 4 Feb 2026. "We have found no amending notification so far." — [secondary] (read) [Mirvo Legal, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/). "The proposal has not been notified", verified 8 Sep 2026: [dcomply](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/) [secondary] (read). One source suggests it may target Significant Data Fiduciaries: [search summary, source unclear] (excerpt). Not confirmed after 20 Sep 2026.
- LAW: Rule 3 notice must stand alone, in clear and plain language. It must give at minimum an itemised description of personal data, the specified purpose and the goods or services enabled. It must also give a link and means to withdraw consent "with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given", to exercise rights, and to complain to the Board. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- LAW: Section 7(a) permits processing for a specified purpose where the person voluntarily provided the data and has not objected. The Act's illustration is a pharmacy customer giving her number for a payment receipt message: the pharmacy "may process the personal data of X for the purpose of sending the receipt". — [secondary reproduction of the Act] (read) [dpdpa.com section 7](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section7.html)
- LAW: Rule 6 sets minimum safeguards: encryption, obfuscation or masking; access control; logs and monitoring; backups; processor contract terms; and logs and personal data retained for one year. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- LAW: Rule 7 breach: tell each affected person without delay. Tell the Board without delay, with details within 72 hours or longer if the Board allows. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). CERT-In reporting is separate: [dcomply](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/rules/) [secondary] (read).
- LAW: Rule 8(3) requires retaining personal data, traffic data and logs for at least one year from processing, then erasure unless another law requires longer. The illustration covers order details being kept even if the account is deleted, and cloud processors holding them too. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- LAW: Rule 8(1)'s three-year inactivity erasure and Third Schedule apply only to named classes: e-commerce entities with at least 2 crore registered users, online gaming intermediaries with at least 50 lakh, social media intermediaries with at least 2 crore. An individual cafe will not be in these. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules, Third Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- LAW: Rule 9 requires published contact information for questions. Rule 14 requires published means for exercising rights. Rule 10 requires verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data. — [official] (read) [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Penalty ceilings: up to ₹250 crore for security failures, up to ₹200 crore for breach reporting or children's data failures. — [secondary] (read) [Mirvo Legal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Separate regime: commercial SMS needs DLT registration of the business, header and templates under TRAI TCCCPR 2018. Promotional and explicit-service messages need consent tied to a template, and mixed messages are usually classified promotional. — [secondary, vendor] (excerpt) [Textlocal](https://textlocal.in/?p=27456), [Fyno](https://fyno.io/docs/dlt-sms-templates). TRAI text not read. Digital consent acquisition being mandatory from Feb 2025 is a vendor claim.

### Inferences
- Roles: the cafe is likely the Data Fiduciary for its customer list and breadPOS its Data Processor. If breadPOS runs cross-merchant loyalty or uses numbers for its own purposes, it may become a fiduciary too. The contract should cover Rule 6(1)(f).
- Flow: phone is optional at billing. Separate purposes are presented as separate unticked choices: (a) digital receipt, (b) loyalty points, (c) offers by SMS or WhatsApp. Using (a) does not cover (b) or (c).
- Capture consent with a customer-facing step (screen or OTP or link), with a stored record of notice version, purpose, time and outlet. Do not rely on a cashier ticking a box for the customer.
- Withdrawal: one-tap link in messages, plus a cashier-assisted path, with the same ease as sign-up. Withdrawal stops marketing but does not delete invoices.
- Retention: separate the customer profile from tax documents. Invoices stay for tax-law periods. Erase or anonymise the profile on request, or after a merchant-set inactivity period. Keep processing logs at least one year.
- Security: mask phone numbers on staff screens and reports, restrict export, log access, and do not print the full number on bills.
- Children: loyalty sign-up needs an adult self-declaration at least, with legal input on what "verifiable" means in-store.
- Language: offer Eighth Schedule languages, which secondary sources say must be available on request.

### Gaps
- Whether any exemption helps small businesses or startups (section 17(3) of the Act allows the government to notify some). None was found for cafes.
- No official or practitioner source reviewed specifically addresses restaurant loyalty, or whether a phone number alone (without a name) changes the analysis.
- Whether WhatsApp marketing falls under TCCCPR. Unanswered.
- How the "one year minimum retention" interacts with a request for erasure beyond the illustration in Rule 8(3). A legal question.
- MeitY's Jan 2026 proposal document itself was not read, and the "Eighth Schedule languages on request" requirement is from secondary sources. It rests on section 5(3) of the Act, which was not read.
