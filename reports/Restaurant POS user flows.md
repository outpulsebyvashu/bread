# Build the running check before adding features

Leading restaurant POS apps organise every role around one shared object: a table holds a running check, each round fires as a KOT routed to a station, a bill is issued from the check, and tenders close it. breadPOS has no such object yet. Its `place_order` RPC inserts a new order with its own tax and total for every round, so table transfer, merge, item-level split, item-level void, course hold and a single table bill have nothing to attach to. The second structural gap is India-specific: the schema has no invoice document, so it cannot yet produce consecutive per-financial-year serials, CGST/SGST lines, GSTIN and FSSAI details, or credit notes linked to an original bill. The third is confirmation: `take_payment` records UPI as paid when a row is inserted, with no pending state, while the workable pattern seen in payment-vendor docs is a per-bill dynamic QR confirmed by the provider. Evidence is uneven and that matters for how far to trust the comparison. Toast, Square and Lightspeed K-Series have readable help centres, Petpooja has video tutorials, Restroworks has product pages only, and **no GST invoicing flow was verified on any Indian dine-in POS**. Nobody tested any product hands-on. I recommend 34 must-haves across the six roles, led by the running check, the invoice document, inline manager approval and a pending UPI state. Legal and tax points below are product design input, not legal or tax advice, and a CA or lawyer should confirm them before they are hard-coded.

## Every vendor converges on table, check, round and tender

Six patterns recur across vendors with readable evidence, and they are the safest to copy. First, **the table is a container for a check, and seats and courses are optional layers**. Toast tiles show guest count, timer, spend and server initials ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Managing-Tables)), Petpooja table cards show start time, item count and amount ([third-party demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y12pNLvkMt8)), and Lightspeed keeps seats and courses switchable ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051089273-Adding-orders-in-Table-Service-mode)). Second, **rounds accumulate on one running order**: Petpooja staff reopen the table, add items and send another KOT ([third-party demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y12pNLvkMt8)), and Toast makes sending a per-item state, with Send, Stay and Hold ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Experience-Ordering-Screens)).

Third, **routing follows item-to-station mapping, set up separately from the menu's live state**. Petpooja assigns each printer by category, item, table, order type, POS station or brand ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRv-9Ofa6F0)), and Square's kitchen routing categories do not disturb menu or reporting categories ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8170-filter-orders-by-category-with-square-kds)). Fourth, **void and comp are separate acts, decided by whether the food was made or delivered**. Square, TouchBistro and Revel state this rule in similar terms ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6429-check-management-with-square-for-restaurants), [TouchBistro docs, excerpt](https://cdn.touchbistro.com/help/articles/using-discounts-and-voids-to-correct-errors/), [Revel docs, excerpt](https://support.revelsystems.com/hc/en-us/articles/204576105-Table-Service-Restaurant-TSR-Point-of-Sale-Guide)). Fifth, **a missing permission turns into a second person's code at the point of action**, not a separate queue: Lightspeed L-Series shows a lock icon and an "Authorization Needed" PIN popup ([Lightspeed docs](https://resto-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/226406048-Using-elevated-permissions)), and Toast prompts a manager passcode on split and discount ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Splitting-Checks-by-Item-1492811097734)). Sixth, **close is a checklist that blocks on open work**, as on Toast and Square ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Close-Out-Day-Z-Report-Auto-Capture), [Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6594-end-of-day-reporting-with-square-for-restaurants)).

Vendors genuinely diverge on four choices, and breadPOS has to pick rather than copy. QR orders go straight to the kitchen on Toast ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Mobile-Order-and-Pay-Overview)) and Restroworks ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/qr-code-ordering-system/)), but wait for confirmation on Petpooja ([Petpooja marketing](https://www.petpooja.com/scan-and-order)) and Lightspeed ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260803553189-Placing-a-table-order)). Loss control leans towards review after the event on Petpooja's leakage tile and Restroworks' flag queue, and towards prevention at the moment of action on Toast and L-Series. Stock is deducted "when an item is billed" on Restroworks ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/restaurant-pos-system/)), on send or after payment on Toast depending on a setting ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Setting-the-Stock-Status-and-Count-for-Menu-Items?lang=en_US)), and ambiguously on Petpooja, whose tutorial says "punched and billed" ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YwrUf0RQR-Y)). Offline depends on architecture, from Lightspeed's local server to Square's reportedly cloud-dependent KDS.

## How each role's flow is structured

### Customer QR: payment timing and staff confirmation are separate decisions

Table-specific QR with no app and no sign-up is the norm where documented. The disagreement is over the gate. Petpooja's own tutorial describes a captain-generated PIN for table QR, and OTP for pickup and general dine-in ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whN-Ot-vhTs)), while its product page has a captain validate an OTP ([Petpooja marketing](https://www.petpooja.com/scan-and-order)) and a blog has staff check an on-screen code ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/what-is-scan-and-order-qr-ordering-indian-restaurants/)). All three describe validation before the order proceeds, and the product page adds that reordering on the same QR joins the same running bill. Lightspeed offers pay online or pay before leaving, shows the order as pending until the restaurant confirms, and warns that enabling pay-in-person means accepting walk-out risk ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260803521850-Managing-Payment-settings)). Pay-first evidence for DotPe is a 2020 press article ([third-party](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/feature/with-nrais-blessing-this-fintech-start-up-is-dreaming-big-should-restaurant-aggregators-be-worried-5964)), and Restroworks says "scan, order, and pay" while also listing cash ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/qr-code-ordering-system/)). The lightest option is pay-only QR on a printed bill, as in Square Scan to Pay ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8456)). Toast also lets a server start the check and guests join it, paying for their own items or the whole check ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Mobile-Order-and-Pay-Overview)).

### Captain: per-user rights, not fixed roles, on Indian products

Petpooja's captain is a phone client of the outlet POS. The rights named in a third-party walkthrough are per-user toggles, including item edit, generate KOT, move table, cancel KOT, generate bill and take payment, and that video reports the app working on the outlet's Wi-Fi rather than mobile data ([third-party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A4eY7Jes6Yw)). Its tutorial confirms that a captain can accept digital payment or cash at billing ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pe61ZTYphnM)). Table status colours distinguish empty, running, KOT printed and paid-but-still-cooking ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0THG1AG7UCM)), and the last state is one breadPOS's table map lacks. Sending has two buttons: KOT digitally, or KOT and print ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfziggfQZYI)).

Table transfer is best documented on global products. Toast uses long-press, select check, destination, confirm, and prompts to merge or keep separate on an occupied table ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/New-POS-Managing-Tables)). Square drags one table onto another, confirming on occupied targets, but merge proper is limited to Register in one article ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6429-check-management-with-square-for-restaurants), [Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8583-manage-seats-in-your-restaurant)). Revel's excerpted merge cannot be undone, which is the failure to avoid ([Revel docs, excerpt](https://support.revelsystems.com/hc/en-us/articles/204576105-Table-Service-Restaurant-TSR-Point-of-Sale-Guide)). Petpooja's two move-table methods come from a 2023 third-party video ([third-party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1v8HsOa2UM)), plus a vendor-documented setting that prints a new KOT with the new table number ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRv-9Ofa6F0)).

Course firing is well documented globally and probably excess for a cafe as a default. Toast offers Required, Optional or Off, with firing by server, timer, previous course completion or expediter ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Course-Firing-Options)). Square uses fire or hold per course ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7748-coursing-with-square-kds)). Petpooja's POS has a documented Hold Order ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0THG1AG7UCM)), and only its blog claims course-by-course firing from a held "wishlist" ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/fine-dine-pos-what-handle-on-busy-floor/)). A per-item hold covers the common cafe case of "mains after starters" without a course model. Modifier rules are thinly evidenced: the only demonstration of enforced minimum and maximum selection is a third-party Petpooja demo where save is blocked until the required number is chosen ([third-party demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y12pNLvkMt8)).

### Kitchen: station screens, simple bump and a recall window

Restroworks' page gives the richest action list: stage flows, mirror groups, bump at ticket, item or combo level, recall, course hold, and a diagnostic that flags unmapped items before service ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/kitchen-display-system/)). That diagnostic is cheap and worth copying. Documented behaviour is simpler. Toast has prep stations and an optional expediter, oldest tickets on the left, and whole-ticket or single-item fulfil ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-With-the-Kitchen-Display-System?lang=en_US)). Square's undo lasts about three seconds ([Square docs, excerpt](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8171-complete-orders-with-square-kds)) and Lightspeed's five ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/22708154090267-Using-the-Kitchen-Display-System-2-0)). Lightspeed echoes statuses back to the POS. In India, printed KOTs remain central: Petpooja can print only modified items, deleted items on a separate KOT, a "duplicate" stamp on reprints, and a barcode that marks food ready for Swiggy handover ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRv-9Ofa6F0)). Those change-KOT settings are the most directly usable Indian kitchen evidence.

Availability is a live-shift task with its own controls. Square's long-press sold-out resets at the end of the business day by default ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6425-managing-items-with-square-for-restaurants)), which prevents a stale 86 silently killing tomorrow's sales. Petpooja's item off asks for 2, 4, 8 or 24 hours, indefinite, or custom ([third-party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qs9WHTZYi4I)), and its tutorial says the KDS itself can mark an item unavailable ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nhAVcwIwWJM)). Toast requires a manager-level passcode and blocks 86ing offline ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/86-an-Item), [Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Using-Toast-in-Offline-Mode?lang=en_US)). KDS-side 86 was not documented for any global product read.

### Cashier: one settlement screen with many tenders and no unassigned rupee

Petpooja's billing separates Save, Save and Print, and Save and e-Bill ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfziggfQZYI)), with tenders of Cash, Card, Due, Other, More and Part, plus split by percentage or item ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lTWIwcqRGyM)). A third-party demo adds that cash underpayment goes to "waived off", and that Due needs a customer number and builds a running balance ([third-party demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y12pNLvkMt8)). Petpooja's blog says a bill cannot settle while any amount is "Not Paid" ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/restaurant-day-end-report-how-close-and-reconcile/)). Complimentary and sales return are explicit bill types ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lTWIwcqRGyM)), though the credit-note treatment is unverified.

Split detail is deepest at Toast and Square, including hazards. Toast has tap-and-drop, split evenly and split by seat, but item-level discounts and service charges can be lost on moves, and one label, "Split evenly", means two different things ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Splitting-Checks-by-Item-1492811097734)). Splitting before sending creates separate kitchen tickets, while Petpooja's blog says the kitchen keeps one set after a split ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/fine-dine-pos-what-handle-on-busy-floor/)). Lightspeed warns that four shared beers across four guests become 0.25 per check ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051089493-Splitting-a-bill)). Bill merge is documented only globally: Toast's Combine Checks picks a destination first and is limited to open, unpaid checks ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Combining-Checks-1492892550423)).

UPI is the widest gap between Indian importance and Indian documentation. On Petpooja's third-party demo UPI is simply a tag under Other, and the cashier can switch a printed bill's tender before settling ([third-party demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y12pNLvkMt8)). Its blog claims integrated payments mark bills settled "without a manual tick", without naming a provider or showing the screen ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/must-have-integrations-for-managing-your-restaurant/)). Restroworks shows a dynamic QR with the amount fixed for that check, without naming UPI or the confirmation path ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/customer-order-display/)). The only concrete mechanics come from a payment vendor: Razorpay generates per-payment QRs with `close_by` at least 15 minutes after creation, and confirmation by polling or webhook ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/upi-qr/api.md)), and its dynamic soundbox matches transactions to invoices ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/pos/dynamic-soundbox.md)).

Shift close on Toast is a strict ordered gate of close checks, declare tips, reconcile, close drawer, clock out, with a manager approving variances over a threshold ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Shift-Review-Checklist-for-Employees-and-Managers)). Tip declaration is US-driven and does not transfer. Lightspeed L-Series is the compact model: amount, reason, then Add cash or Final count ([Lightspeed docs](https://resto-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/226306047-Cash-drawer-start-and-final-counts)). Square records Pay In and Out with a description, as a paid add-on ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8344-start-and-end-a-cash-drawer-session)).

### Manager: inline approvals, reason codes and a leakage view

Toast's void flow is the clearest reference. It separates void, comp and refund, allows voiding a captured payment on the same business day and only refunding after that ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/article/Voiding-Items-Payments-and-Checks)), and, once any reason is active, prompts staff from a preset list with no free text ([Toast docs, excerpt](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Add-Void-Reasons)). Items unsent can be removed without a void. Lightspeed K-Series logs user, table, item, date and reason ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050328554-Editing-and-reviewing-orders)). TouchBistro has the manager enter a code on the server's session rather than logging in, which keeps the audit trail on the original user ([TouchBistro docs, excerpt](https://cdn.touchbistro.com/help/articles/voiding-an-item-2/)).

Indian evidence points to detection rather than prevention. Petpooja's dashboard has a documented leakage tile covering bills modified, reprinted or waived off, and KOTs cancelled, modified or not converted to bills ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ReK9on1tAXc)). The only in-the-moment control seen is an owner's password plus typed reason on bill cancel, from one third-party video ([third-party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2FGgvbIIERE)). Restroworks flags voids, deletes, discounts, reprints and comps with risk scores, treating a void two minutes after print as a correction and 45 minutes after as a decision, and records a Doer and an Owner ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/restaurant-anti-theft-software/)). Its "Approve" reviews flags after the event and should not be mistaken for pre-approval. For a cafe where the owner is nearby or on a phone, the two approaches complement each other: PIN at the moment for high-risk acts, and a leakage view for the rest.

Day close is best documented on Toast, with an automatic 4 a.m. cutoff, an optional audit screen, and a Z report that prints without ending the day ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Close-Out-Day-Z-Report-Auto-Capture)). Petpooja claims a manual business-day close for outlets trading past midnight ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/must-try-features-petpooja-pos/)), and Lightspeed's default business day runs 5:30 AM to 5:29 AM ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050328874-About-POS-reports)). Square and Revel refuse to close offline ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6594-end-of-day-reporting-with-square-for-restaurants), [Revel docs, excerpt](https://support.revelsystems.com/hc/en-us/articles/204163039-How-to-Set-Up-and-Run-the-End-of-Day-Process)), while Petpooja says day-end works offline ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/restaurant-pos-without-internet/)).

### Owner or admin: recipes, variance and setup are the back-office core

Petpooja's inventory is its best-documented area: units, raw materials with purchase and consumption units, semi-finished goods, then recipes that also cover add-ons and variations ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3jFViCCtMPE)), followed by a closing stock entry without which variance cannot be generated ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7BoHRhPwYBU)). Modifier-level deduction is the differentiator for a cafe, where milk type and extra shot are modifiers. Square's beta explicitly excludes it, and also cannot take recipes on multi-variation items ([Square docs](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8629-beta-track-ingredient-costs-with-square-recipes)). Square's auto sold-out with staff override is the right posture for imprecise counts. Restroworks logs waste with a reason code and phone counts ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/restaurant-inventory-management-software/)). Lightspeed's made-to-order versus made-in-batches split is a simple later step for in-house baking ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4407511552155-Creating-and-managing-recipes)). Reporting expectations are shaped by Petpooja's dashboard tiles: sales by order type, user and payment mode, discounts by user, cancelled and complimentary counts, taxes collected and sync status ([Petpooja tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ReK9on1tAXc)). Permission depth varies from Toast's numbered list ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Access-Permissions-Reference)) to preset groups on Lightspeed ([Lightspeed docs](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804594690-Managing-POS-users)), and a small cafe needs presets plus a few toggles.

### Cross-role: aggregators, loyalty and offline

UrbanPiper's documentation provides a usable aggregator state machine: forward-only statuses, acknowledgement required before Food Ready, and Indian aggregator-delivered orders completed only by the aggregator ([UrbanPiper docs](https://api-docs.urbanpiper.com/downstream/api/endpoints/order-management/order-status-update.md)). Cancellation is a request that can fail: a disallowed Swiggy or Zomato cancel falls back to a callback, and the docs say not to retry. Menu changes go through push, per-aggregator validation and a publish step ([UrbanPiper docs](https://api-docs.urbanpiper.com/downstream/menu/validation-and-publish.md)), and stock toggles are asynchronous with an expected but unguaranteed 30 seconds ([UrbanPiper docs](https://api-docs.urbanpiper.com/downstream/api/endpoints/menu/menu-toggle.md)). Petpooja's operator flow runs accept or reject, Food is Ready, then rider collection and automatic settlement, per a 2023 walkthrough ([third-party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qs9WHTZYi4I)). Toast's Uber Eats docs show the POS as menu source of truth with a markup option ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Getting-Started-Uber-Eats-Integration)). None of this is worth building before a small cafe's dine-in flows work, but the channel field should go in now.

Loyalty patterns favour redemption before tender and customer-side consent. Toast applies the reward as a discount before tender so it works for any payment type, and states that guests must sign up themselves because the restaurant cannot consent on their behalf ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Redeeming-Points-with-Toast-Loyalty), [Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/How-Guests-Enroll-in-Toast-Loyalty)). Petpooja claims OTP-protected redemption so staff cannot drain a wallet unnoticed ([Petpooja marketing, search summary](https://www.petpooja.com/poss/restaurant-loyalty-program)). Restroworks moves points only after the guest approves a join prompt on the customer display ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/customer-order-display/)). Toast also warns that split payments do not accrue points correctly across accounts, so split the check instead ([Toast docs](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Splitting-Checks-by-Item-1492811097734)).

Offline is table stakes in Indian positioning and the softest claim in the evidence. Petpooja markets up to 5 days on most builds, with billing, KOT printing, table management, cash settlement and day-end continuing, and aggregators, EDC card payments, cloud reports and menu edits stopping, with direct UPI QR suggested as a fallback ([Petpooja marketing](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/restaurant-pos-without-internet/)). Restroworks claims 48 hours at the counter ([Restroworks marketing](https://www.restroworks.com/cafe-management-system/)), Rista "days" ([store text](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.ristaapps.business)), and no independent reliability evidence was found for any of them. Documented global behaviour shows the real costs. ## Gaps show the draft breaks its own flows in several places

This section reads `docs/USERFLOWS.md` against `supabase/schema.sql` by inspection only. The schema is an untested draft and I did not run it. `docs/ROADMAP.md` already lists table state, required-modifier validation, per-outlet stock, order numbering and item-level split as known gaps, so those are not new findings. The draft does get several things right that vendors treat as important: tax computed after discount, server-side pricing, no service-charge line, phone optional, an outbox so Niro outages cannot block billing, and a repeating KOT alert until accepted.

Several gaps are defects against the written flows rather than missing features, and these should be fixed first. **Kitchen cannot mark an item sold out**: the flow says it can, but `items` writes are limited to manager and admin by RLS, and no RPC exists. **Kitchen can read prices and payments**: the flow says it cannot, but the "staff read" policy on `orders`, `order_items`, `payments` and `shifts` admits any active staff, and `orders` and `order_items` also feed realtime. **A cashier can bypass the discount cap**: `set_discount` skips the cap whenever `p_niro_ref` is non-null and never validates it, so any string works if the RPC is called directly. **An anonymous caller can attach any member to an order**: `place_order` accepts `p_niro_member` from the client, and `take_payment` then queues an earn for that member. **Reason is "mandatory" only on paper**: `cancel_order` accepts an empty reason. **Managers settle outside the drawer**: `take_payment` only requires an open shift for cashiers, so a manager's cash has a null `shift_id` and escapes every variance check. **Cancelling always restores stock**, even for food already cooked. Finally, `take_payment` does not refuse cancelled or already-paid orders or overpayment, and `set_discount` does not refuse paid orders.

## Recommended flow changes for a small Indian cafe

The ranking favours flows that block others, protect money or carry legal exposure, then daily speed. Must-have means needed before a real outlet runs on breadPOS. Should-have means within the first months, or when the outlet's setup needs it. Later means real but deferrable, or only for larger formats. Evidence strength for each change is shown as **Strong** (several vendor docs), **Medium** (one vendor doc or legal source), **Weak** (marketing or third-party) or **Inference** (my design judgement, including the small-cafe defaults). Items marked CA need confirmation from a practising CA. Per `CLAUDE.md`, changes to money or permission logic and any stack change need the owner's approval first; this report recommends and changes nothing.

Across roles, the build order should be: fix the defects listed above, then G1 and G2 (check and KOT), G5 and G4 (tax and invoice), G7 and G8 (approvals and voids), then G9 and G10 (UPI and cash), because the later ones all reference the earlier records. The current roadmap builds cashier payments in phase 3 and captain flows in phase 6. The check and invoice model should move into phase 1 or 2, or phase 3 will be rebuilt.

**Offline is the one recommendation that needs an owner decision rather than a backlog slot.** The architecture says v1 requires connectivity and the roadmap lists the queue as optional, while Indian competitors all market offline billing. I would not promise parity on claims nobody has verified. My recommendation: for go-live, make the outage path explicit, with a connectivity banner, no sign-out while unsynced work exists, and a written paper-bill routine with a "back-enter" flow that uses a separate invoice series. Next, promote a single-counter offline queue from optional to should-have, using client-generated IDs, idempotent RPCs and a per-device series. A local hub for KDS and captain over LAN is a later choice and a stack question, since a Supabase-first PWA has no local server. Cash and static UPI are the only tenders likely to work during an outage. This position rests on inference, not on tested vendor behaviour.

## Conclusion

The useful surprise is how little of this is about screens. The six role flows in `USERFLOWS.md` read reasonably against the market, and the gaps sit underneath them, in records the flows assume but the schema lacks. Since no app code exists yet, these are cheap to fix today and expensive after phase 3. The most valuable sequence is therefore a data-model pass before the first cashier screen, along with a security fix for the flow-versus-RLS mismatches and the two Niro trust holes.

The evidence also suggests where breadPOS can be better rather than merely equal. Vendors' documented weak spots are specific and avoidable: Toast's 86 needing a manager and failing offline, Revel's irreversible merge, Square's recipes ignoring modifiers, and a single auto-fire device as a point of failure. Combined with a provider-confirmed UPI flow that no Indian vendor publicly documents, and a consent step built ahead of the DPDP dates, a small POS can lead on exactly the things a cafe owner feels daily. The next step that would most reduce uncertainty is a one-week hands-on trial of Petpooja and one other Indian POS, with a test bill, a split, a void and a UPI failure, because those are the flows this research could only read about.
